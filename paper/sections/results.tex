\subsection{RQ2: What makes the problem hard, and does the policy transfer?}
\label{sec:rq2}

\textbf{Map--observation change.}
In the hand-crafted descriptor setting, localization success drops monotonically with the visual change between the pre-event map and the post-event observation across all methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:hard}a). From the least to the most changed quintile in Taquari, the straight-line heuristic falls from \vOneSuccessQOneStraight\% to \vOneSuccessQFiveStraight\%, while the PPO policy declines more gradually from \vOneSuccessQOnePpo\% to \vOneSuccessQFivePpo\%. An independent Lab color-difference covariate confirms this degradation trend without relying on the descriptor itself (Spearman $\rho$ from \vOneRhoColorMin{} to \vOneRhoColorMax). We also tested the hypothesis that false localizations occur because the policy is drawn toward cells that appear falsely distinctive on the outdated map; this mechanism was \vOneMOneVerdict. Although episodes ending in false localization traverse more altered terrain than successful ones (+\vOneMOneChangeDiff{} mean change index, 95\% CI $[\vOneMOneChangeDiffLow; \vOneMOneChangeDiffHigh]$), the visited cells are neither more distinctive on the pre-event map nor more changed than those traversed by the straight-line baseline.

\textbf{Zero-shot spatial transfer.}
Transferred zero-shot from Taquari S2 to Porto Alegre (February 2024 map), the hand-crafted PPO policy matches the straight-line heuristic in the urban center (\vOnePoaCenterPpo\% vs.\ \vOnePoaCenterStraight\%, CI $[\vOnePoaCenterDiffLow; +\vOnePoaCenterDiffHigh]$~p.p.), where flooded streets surround structured city blocks, but falls behind on the heavily submerged river islands (\vOnePoaIslandsPpo\% vs.\ \vOnePoaIslandsStraight\%, CI $[\vOnePoaIslandsDiffLow; +\vOnePoaIslandsDiffHigh]$~p.p.).

\subsection{RQ3: Can a preference-conditioned policy control the risk?}
\label{sec:rq3}

In Section~\ref{sec:rq1}, the hand-crafted PPO policy achieved shorter flights than the straight-line heuristic at the cost of a higher false-localization rate (\vOneFalseLocPpoSTwo\% vs.\ \vOneFalseLocStraightSTwo\%). To test whether a single policy conditioned on preference weights can control this efficiency--risk trade-off, we evaluated MOPPO in the same setting. Under our training budget, weight conditioning failed to separate behaviors. The pre-specified conditioning gate—which checks at 25\% of training whether distinct preference vectors induce distinct action distributions—failed during training. When evaluated with greedy (argmax) actions, the policy selected identical moves in 100\% of probed states across the ``uniform'', ``fast'' and ``safe'' presets, producing identical test metrics across 32 of 37 evaluated weight vectors. When re-evaluated with sampled actions (matching the stochastic exploration used during training), the presets differed only marginally. Consequently, our pre-specified hypotheses that preference weights would monotonically control false-localization risk and step cost (\moHSixVerdict) and that the ``safe'' preset would reduce false localizations on the Porto Alegre islands (\moHSevenVerdict) were not supported, while the comparison against separately trained fixed-weight policies was \moHFourVerdict{} due to the failed conditioning gate. Averaged across presets, the expected utility of the conditioned policy (\moEumSampled) did not improve upon unconditioned PPO seeds (\moEumPocSampled). We report this as a negative result: linear weight conditioning on the belief encoder was too weak to steer the stopping--risk trade-off.

\subsection{RQ4: When does learning to act pay off?}
\label{sec:rq4}

\textbf{Perceptual ceiling vs.\ multi-site ambiguity with DINOv2.}
Replacing the hand-crafted features with the fine-tuned DINOv2 descriptor separates the test sites into two regimes (Fig.~\ref{fig:hard}b, Table~\ref{tab:v3baselines}). In Taquari, where the descriptor was fine-tuned on a disjoint spatial band, single-image perception is strong enough that every heuristic exceeds 83\% success: random motion alone reaches \vThreeTaqSTwoRandom\% in S2 and the straight-line heuristic reaches \vThreeTaqSTwoStraight\%. In this regime, active route selection has almost nothing left to add: across all \vThreeMatrixRuns{} PPO training runs (three policy variants across ten seeds each), none passed the pre-specified learning gate (advantage over random motion at 25\% of training between $\vThreeMatrixAdvMin$ and $\vThreeMatrixAdvMax$~p.p.), yielding no verdict on the corresponding pre-specified hypotheses (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). In the zero-shot sites of Canoas and Porto Alegre, however, perceptual ambiguity returns: random-walk success drops to \vThreeCanoasRandom\% (Canoas), \vThreeIslandsRandom\% (islands) and \vThreeCenterRandom\% (center), whereas the straight-line heuristic maintains \vThreeCanoasStraight\%, \vThreeIslandsStraight\% and \vThreeCenterStraight\%. Flying the straight line at 150~m altitude helps most where perception is hardest (\vThreeCanoasStraightHigh\% vs.\ \vThreeCanoasStraight\% in Canoas; \vThreeIslandsStraightHigh\% vs.\ \vThreeIslandsStraight\% on the islands), whereas greedy information gain—which predicts observations from the outdated map—is the weakest informed strategy everywhere, falling below random motion in Canoas (\vThreeCanoasGreedy\% vs.\ \vThreeCanoasRandom\%) and tying with it on the islands (\vThreeIslandsGreedy\% vs.\ \vThreeIslandsRandom\%). Planning on what the outdated map predicts is the wrong inductive bias after a flood.

\textbf{Controlled perceptual ambiguity.}
To isolate how perceptual ambiguity governs the value of a learned policy within the same DINOv2 environment, we injected isotropic Gaussian noise of scale $\sigma$ into the normalized observation descriptors of Taquari (fold~A) while keeping the map descriptors, reward function and training budget fixed (five seeds per level). Noise levels were calibrated solely from random-walk success on \emph{training} windows prior to policy training. Because the histogram filter integrates evidence over dozens of steps and leverages window-boundary geometry, random-walk success remains above \eMaxSigmaScanThree\% for $\sigma\le 3$ even after single-image top-1 retrieval drops from \eTopOneLowSigma\% ($\sigma=\eLowSigma$) to nearly zero. Scanning \eNSigmas{} noise scales yielded three levels L1--L3 ($\sigma=\eSigmaLOne,\eSigmaLTwo,\eSigmaLThree$) with training-window random-walk success of \eRandTrainLOne\%, \eRandTrainLTwo\% and \eRandTrainLThree\%, alongside the noise-free baseline L0 (\eRandTrainLZero\%). Because this experiment is exploratory, all runs were evaluated on the test set regardless of the learning gate.

\textbf{The policy learns to beat unguided motion.}
As perceptual ambiguity increases from L0 to L3, the success advantage of belief-space PPO over random motion grows monotonically (Fig.~\ref{fig:e}, blue curve): from $\eAdvRandLZero$~p.p.\ $[\eAdvRandLZeroLow; \eAdvRandLZeroHigh]$ at L0 to $\eAdvRandLOne$~p.p.\ at L1, $\eAdvRandLTwo$~p.p.\ at L2 and $\eAdvRandLThree$~p.p.\ $[\eAdvRandLThreeLow; \eAdvRandLThreeHigh]$ at L3. Correspondingly, the fraction of seeds passing the learning gate rises from \eGateLZero{} at L0 and \eGateLOne{} at L1 to \eGateLTwo{} at L2 and \eGateLThree{} at L3. Learning where to fly begins to pay off against random motion precisely as single-image perception degrades.

\textbf{Why systematic coverage still wins.}
On training windows, the strongest heuristic at every noise level was the \eBestName{}. Because it continuously traverses fresh terrain with a wide camera footprint, it is remarkably resilient to observation noise, declining only from \eSuccBestLZero\% at L0 to \eSuccBestLThree\% at L3 (Table~\ref{tab:e}). Over the same range, PPO success drops from \eSuccPpoLZero\% to \eSuccPpoLThree\%, causing its gap relative to the best heuristic to widen from $\eAdvBestLZero$~p.p.\ $[\eAdvBestLZeroLow; \eAdvBestLZeroHigh]$ at L0 to $\eAdvBestLThree$~p.p.\ $[\eAdvBestLThreeLow; \eAdvBestLThreeHigh]$ at L3 (Fig.~\ref{fig:e}, orange curve). Our pre-specified hypothesis that the policy's advantage over the best heuristic would grow from L0 to L3 is therefore \eThreeVerdict{} ($\Delta(\mathrm{L3})-\Delta(\mathrm{L0})=\eThree$~p.p., $[\eThreeLow; \eThreeHigh]$; argmax: $\eThreeArgmax$~p.p., $[\eThreeArgmaxLow; \eThreeArgmaxHigh]$). This gap is not merely an artifact of camera altitude: the PPO policies fly at 100~m, yet at L3 both the straight line at 100~m (\eSuccStraightLThree\%) and the lawnmower sweep (\eSuccLawnmowerLThree\%) also outperform PPO (\eSuccPpoLThree\%), while greedy information gain trails closely (\eSuccGreedyLThree\%). Under high ambiguity, PPO also incurs a false-localization rate comparable to random motion (\eFalseLocPpoLThree\% vs.\ \eFalseLocRandLThree\%).

\textbf{Real ambiguity: zero-shot transfer to Canoas.}
We initially planned a within-site train/test evaluation in Canoas, the hardest real site, but its post-event mosaic contains only \eCanoasWindows{} valid windows clustered in a single corner, precluding disjoint spatial splits. As a descriptive check on real post-flood ambiguity, we evaluated the ten noise-free Taquari (L0) policies zero-shot across all Canoas windows. They achieve \eZsPpo\% success $[\eZsPpoLow; \eZsPpoHigh]$: substantially outperforming random motion ($\eZsDiffRandom$~p.p., $[\eZsDiffRandomLow; \eZsDiffRandomHigh]$) and greedy information gain ($\eZsDiffGreedy$~p.p., $[\eZsDiffGreedyLow; \eZsDiffGreedyHigh]$), matching the lawnmower sweep ($\eZsDiffLawnmower$~p.p., $[\eZsDiffLawnmowerLow; \eZsDiffLawnmowerHigh]$), and trailing the straight line at 100~m ($\eZsDiffStraight$~p.p., $[\eZsDiffStraightLow; \eZsDiffStraightHigh]$) and at 150~m ($\eZsDiffAltStraight$~p.p., $[\eZsDiffAltStraightLow; \eZsDiffAltStraightHigh]$). Synthetic noise in Taquari and real flood ambiguity in Canoas produce the exact same ranking of methods.

\subsection{Real-UAV Validation and Real-Time Feasibility}
\label{sec:uav}

To verify that our findings hold beyond satellite-to-satellite imagery, we validate perception, filtering and active control on real UAV imagery from two sources in southern Brazil, and benchmark onboard CPU latency.

\textbf{Active localization over a real post-flood UAV orthomosaic.}
We assemble three open-data post-flood UAV RGB orthomosaics captured by the S\~ao Paulo Fire Department (CBPMESP) on May 25--26, 2024 over Mathias Velho, Canoas ($4.1$--$15.1$~cm GSD, $5.17\text{ km}^2$ overlap, $<5$~m residual alignment against the pre-flood February 2024 Maxar map)~\cite{cbpmesp_oam_2024}. Using the exact same Taquari-fine-tuned DINOv2 descriptor (\texttt{ftA}), PCA projection and zero-shot filter calibration $\beta$, single-image retrieval with realistic sensor perturbations yields $5.0\%$ exact top-1 ($15.4\%$ within 100~m across 5{,}435 cells; $49.5\%$ exact top-1 zero-shot \texttt{grid4} on unperturbed 75~m-spaced cells). We evaluate 1{,}000 paired episodes across 64 valid $1.0\times 1.0$~km ($40\times 40$-cell) windows fully covered by the UAV mosaic (padding $40\times 40 \to 128\times 128$ for the ten zero-shot Taquari PPO checkpoints). Table~\ref{tab:uav} confirms the exact same core conclusions under real UAV-to-satellite domain shift: systematic coverage at 100~m (\emph{straight}: $35.5\%\;[30.7;\;40.2]$; \emph{lawnmower}: $25.3\%$) decisively leads with $0.0\%$ false localizations, whereas planning on the outdated map (\emph{greedy\_map}: $4.4\%$) collapses well below \emph{random} ($11.9\%$). Zero-shot PPO ($11.7\%$ sampled, $9.1\%$ argmax) does not surpass random motion under the combined cross-view and window-size shift. Notably, in $1.0$~km windows \emph{straight} at 100~m outperforms \emph{straight 150~m} ($18.1\%$), as climbing costs 15~s and the 270~m footprint reduces distinct views within 1~km.

\textbf{Raw drone imagery and passive filtering.}
To test unrectified onboard camera frames (real lens distortion, attitude and yaw), we evaluate zero-shot DINOv2 \texttt{grid4} on three real flights of a DJI Matrice 100 with a Parrot Sequoia RGB camera over a campus site in Porto Alegre (~100~m) and Arroio do Meio in the Taquari valley (ADM1, 60--191~m; ADM2, 79--103~m) against multi-year satellite maps (2002--2018). On crisp maps with moderate age, single-image top-1 reaches $78.6\%$ (Porto Alegre campus, 2012 map) and $42.9\%$ (ADM2, 2014 map), and our passive Bayesian histogram filter (using relative odometry without GNSS) converges to $23.5$~m and $8.5$~m final error, respectively. On severely outdated or blurred maps (campus 2002/2018, ADM1/ADM2 2016--2018), single-image top-1 falls into $23.3\%$--$43.3\%$—directly anchoring the perceptual-ambiguity regime studied in Taquari S2 ($33.2\%$) and Section~\ref{sec:rq4} in real UAV flights.

\textbf{Real-time CPU feasibility.}
Benchmarking one complete decision step on a 4-thread CPU without GPU yields $106.2\pm 6.1$~ms (p95: $115.9$~ms) for DINOv2 ViT-S/14 (\texttt{grid4} + projection), $2.14\pm 0.60$~ms for the $128\times 128$ histogram filter update, and $0.90\pm 0.42$~ms for the PPO CNN forward pass: $109.2$~ms total (p95: $121.2$~ms), consuming only $1.82\%$ of the 6.0~s flight-step budget.
