\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ~0,2 pág. Respostas às 4 RQs e trabalhos futuros.

We asked when learning where to fly pays off for UAV localization over outdated post-flood maps. With ambiguous perception, a belief-space PPO policy matches a straight-line heuristic in success and is more efficient, but riskier (RQ1). Localization gets harder as the map departs from the ground, and planning on the old map is the weakest informed strategy (RQ2). Conditioning one policy on preference weights did not give control over the efficiency--risk trade-off (RQ3). When perception is strong, the policy has nothing to add; as perception becomes ambiguous, it increasingly beats motion without a plan, but a straight line flown high degrades even less, and in our setting stays ahead; at the hardest level, even the straight line at the altitude of the policy succeeds more often (\eSuccStraightLThree\% vs.\ \eSuccPpoLThree\%) (RQ4). Validation on real post-flood UAV orthomosaics in Canoas and raw drone flights confirms these findings under real cross-view domain shift while running in $109.2$~ms per step on CPU.

The results point to what a learned policy would need to be worth its cost here: rewards or inputs that value coverage of new terrain, altitude as an action, and larger training budgets. A policy that \emph{chooses among} coverage heuristics, rather than replacing them, is a natural next step.
