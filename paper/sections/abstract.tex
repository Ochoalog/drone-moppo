\begin{abstract}
% Números só por macro (paper/numbers.tex).
A UAV that loses GNSS after a flood must localize itself against reference imagery captured before the disaster, when flooded roads and shifted riverbanks no longer match the map. We study when \emph{learning where to fly} pays off in this active visual localization problem. Following the Active Neural Localizer, we track the drone's position with a Bayesian histogram filter and train belief-space PPO policies on real satellite imagery from before and after the May~2024 floods in southern Brazil, comparing them against five motion heuristics on paired episodes with hierarchical confidence intervals over training seeds and map windows. Under a weak hand-crafted descriptor, the learned policy matches a straight-line heuristic in success (\vOneSuccessPpoSTwo\% vs.\ \vOneSuccessStraightSTwo\%) while flying one-third less distance, but incurs more false localizations (\vOneFalseLocPpoSTwo\% vs.\ \vOneFalseLocStraightSTwo\%); a preference-conditioned multi-objective variant failed to control this risk. Under a contrastively fine-tuned DINOv2 descriptor, perception largely solves the training site on its own (random motion reaches \vThreeTaqSTwoRandom\% success and none of \vThreeMatrixRuns{} PPO runs pass our pre-specified learning gate), whereas on harder zero-shot sites greedy information gain over the outdated map is the weakest informed strategy. In an exploratory experiment that injects controlled observation noise, the policy's advantage over random motion grows monotonically from $\eAdvRandLZero$ to $\eAdvRandLThree$ percentage points (\eGateLThree{} runs passing the gate at the hardest level). However, straight-line flight at the highest altitude degrades even less, widening its lead over the policy from $\eAdvBestLZero$ to $\eAdvBestLThree$ points, and even a straight line at the policy's own altitude remains ahead at the hardest level (\eSuccStraightLThree\% vs.\ \eSuccPpoLThree\%). Validating on real post-flood UAV orthomosaics of Canoas (CBPMESP, May 2024) against the pre-flood satellite map confirms the same ranking: straight-line coverage leads ($35.5\%$, $0.0\%$ false localizations) while greedy planning collapses ($4.4\%$) below random motion ($11.9\%$) and zero-shot PPO ($11.7\%$). Raw drone flights further anchor this ambiguity regime, and one full decision step runs in $109.2$~ms on CPU ($1.8\%$ of the 6~s flight step). Learning to act increasingly outperforms unguided motion as perceptual ambiguity grows, but in our setting does not surpass systematic straight-line coverage.
\end{abstract}
